Pantanal 2026: the water that left, seen by radar

NISAR L-band radar follows the 2026 dry season on the Paraguay River floodplain, from Corumbá–Ladário to the Mandioré lagoon (Brazil–Bolivia), and is checked against optical satellite water maps and the river gauge at Ladário.

How it was done

Method

NISAR L2 GCOV (provisional, CRID P05023; track 132 ascending, frame 168), HH and HV. Clipped, multilooked from 20 to 40 m in linear power, Lee filter (5×5), converted to dB (gamma0). Fixed thresholds adapted from published PALSAR values for the Pantanal (Evans et al. 2010, sigma0 converted to gamma0), applied to all dates and shifted per date by the backscatter change of a stable reference (forested hills above 200 m). Masks: OpenStreetMap urban areas and Copernicus DEM above 135 m. Patches under 1 ha removed.

Validation

Independent: OPERA DSWx-HLS (Harmonized Landsat–Sentinel-2, 30 m) on the nearest date (≤ 2 days), and the Paraguay River stage at Ladário (ANA station 66825000, 15-min telemetry averaged per day). Control zone: JRC Global Surface Water occurrence > 90%. A pre-registered test of a classifier for partial (vegetated) water from radar backscatter alone failed and is reported as a negative result.

Limitations

Seven to eight dates, consecutive and not independent: p-values are indicative. NISAR products are provisional. An L-band open-water rule does not capture shallow water under emergent or floating vegetation. The Copernicus DEM is a surface model (includes canopy).
